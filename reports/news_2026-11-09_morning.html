Front page

Midterms trigger the AI sell-off, then a partial rebound

Democrats won control of both the Senate and the House in the 3 November midterms, Khaleej Times reported. Exit polls cited there showed about half of voters saw the vote as a chance to rebuke the president, and the war with Iran and the cost of living were named as drivers. Final seat counts were not given, and Alaska and Mississippi were still unresolved in one tally (Super Macro Briefing).

Markets read the result as bad news for AI infrastructure. Investors worried that tighter AI rules, local opposition and permitting hurdles could slow US data-centre construction, IJR reported. On Thursday the S&P 500 fell about 4.4%, the Nasdaq Composite more than 5.5% and the Nasdaq 100 about 6.3%, the steepest one-day fall since April 2025, according to Yahoo Finance. WION put the drop in the Philadelphia chip index at 12.2%, with about $3 trillion wiped off S&P 500 companies and the VIX at 28.18. Nvidia fell sharply: 7.3% according to Crypto Briefing, about 9% according to IJR.

On Friday the S&P 500 rose 0.31% and the Nasdaq 1.09%, while the Dow slipped 0.46%, The Closing Print reported. Chip and AI names bounced: AMD gained 11.04%, and Nvidia closed at $186.64, up 2.77%, per Motley Fool. Breadth stayed weak, with 318 S&P 500 stocks falling against 184 rising. Analysts quoted by IJR say construction delays could postpone orders but that there is no sign yet of customers cancelling, and UBS warned that about 30% of planned US data-centre capacity additions for 2028 could be at risk. The next test is Nvidia's earnings on 17 November, after the US close.

Oil slumps on deal hopes while Hormuz stays dangerous

Brent settled at $98.32 on Friday after falling more than 16% from $117.64 a week earlier, FinanceFeeds reported; other tallies put the weekly fall nearer 13% depending on the contract used (Investomine, HDFC Sky). Brent fell 9.73% on Wednesday alone. The slide came as traders bet that a Democratic Congress would push Washington toward ending the eight-month conflict. Iran has reportedly sent a nuclear proposal to Washington through Qatar, but no agreement has been announced. The same report said Hormuz flows are running below 60% of capacity, and an energy analyst quoted there put the chance of fighting resuming at 80%, with prices possibly rising above $100 again.

The weekend brought more incidents. On Saturday evening explosions were heard in southern Iran, including on Qeshm Island, and Iranian state media suggested they were not US attacks, Oman Observer reported. The Revolutionary Guards said two tankers caught fire after hitting mines on an "unauthorised route"; the US military said it fired on a commercial vessel trying to pass its blockade, beyond the strait. No casualties were reported. Qatar denied reports that talks had failed. Iran says further talks depend on the US meeting seven demands, including sanctions relief, frozen assets and war reparations, while Washington says nuclear issues must be part of any first round.

A hot jobs report revives the rate-hike debate

US employers added 165,000 jobs in October against forecasts of about 59,000–65,000, and unemployment fell to 4.1%, The Closing Print and FactSet show. Average hourly earnings rose 0.4% on the month and 3.1% on the year†. Markets now price roughly an 80% chance of a quarter-point Fed hike at the 8–9 December meeting, up from 67% a day earlier, according to FinanceFeeds; The Gold Investor has about 81%. The Fed held at 3.75–4.00% in October with two officials voting to hike†. The dollar rose about 0.7% on the day and gold reversed lower.

Americas

United States. Beyond the Thursday–Friday swings above, the week's sector picture was stark: energy fell almost 12% and technology nearly 7%, while consumer discretionary, communications and real estate rose, Investomine reported. Ten-year Treasury yields ended the week at 4.86%, 8 basis points lower, and the 30-year at 5.26%†. Consumer data were mixed: Michigan sentiment improved to 51 from 48, with year-ahead inflation expectations easing to 4.3% from 4.7%, and ISM manufacturing rose to 55.5 from 54.5 (same source). Notable Friday movers: Coherent +13.58%, Airbnb +11.67%, AMD +11.04%, Axon −18.18%, Microchip −10.02% (The Closing Print).

Canada. The Bank of Canada raised its overnight rate by 25 basis points to 2.50% on 28 October to stop high energy prices spreading to other goods. It said more increases may be needed, with inflation at 3.1% in September and expected near 3.5% by year-end. The next decision is on 9 December (Rio Times).

Brazil. The central bank held the Selic at 13.75% on 4 November, ending five straight cuts, in a unanimous vote. It cited inflation of 4.58% over twelve months to September, above the 4.5% tolerance ceiling, a weaker real and Middle East oil pressure. The next decision is 9 December, and the Focus survey (9 November), Copom minutes (10 November) and October inflation (12 November) come first (Rio Times).

Mexico: not covered this edition because no reliable source was found.

Washington after the sweep. Democrats won control of both chambers, but counts were still moving in early reports: TD Economics (4 November) cited AP for 228 House and 54 Senate seats, Akin Gump had 237 House seats and a 54–44 Senate with 51 House races uncalled, and Schwab said Democrats were on track for about 250 House seats and 54 to 56 Senate seats. Alaska and Mississippi were too close to call. The new Congress convenes on 3 January 2027, and Hakeem Jeffries and Chuck Schumer are expected to lead. Until then the Republican majorities stay: Congress returns on 9 November, and funding runs out on 11 December, with talks reported on an extension into early 2027 or even to 30 September 2027, though a shutdown is not ruled out. The debt limit of about $41.4 trillion is expected to bind in early to mid-2027.

What it means for policy and markets. TD Economics expects the effect to be modest: big Republican tax and spending plans become harder, Senate confirmations of judges and Fed governors become harder, and Democrats can push to limit presidential tariff powers, but most tariffs rest on powers Congress delegated and a veto override needs two-thirds of both chambers. TD expects US growth to accelerate to 2.5% in 2026 (Q4 on Q4). Schwab notes that the S&P 500 has risen in the six months after every midterm since 1974, by 12.4% on average, and warns that past results are no guarantee. Democrats are also expected to look at data-centre growth, crypto legislation and the administration's business ties. Note the market reaction was not smooth: the AI-led sell-off came the day after those early gains (see the Front page).

Fed week. Ten Fed officials speak this week, their first comments since the sweep, and fed funds futures price a 78% chance of a December hike (Yardeni Research). Yardeni's own projections, which are Cleveland Fed nowcasts, put October CPI at +0.24% m/m headline and +0.21% core, with headline 3.7% y/y and core 2.5%; PCE inflation at 3.8% headline and 3.2% core; and PPI hotter than September's 6.3% headline and 5.0% core. Initial jobless claims last week were 189,000 (four-week average 194,750). The S&P 500 2026 earnings-per-share consensus quoted there is $366.60.

Earnings to watch. Monday after the close: Take-Two, Occidental Petroleum, Rocket Lab. Tuesday: CoreWeave, Petrobras. Thursday: Cisco and Applied Materials. Nvidia reports on 17 November. Consensus figures are in the Week ahead tables (StockTitan).

Background on tariffs. Headline-level only, because the pages could not be read in full: a Greenberg Traurig note from February 2026 is titled "Supreme Court invalidates Trump administration's IEEPA tariffs" (GT Law), and an AP report of 4 March 2026 is titled "Judge rules companies are entitled to refunds for Trump tariffs overturned by the Supreme Court" (Click On Detroit). Other tariff authorities remain in use, per TD above.

Europe

Inflation and the ECB. Euro-area inflation reached 4.0% in October, the highest since 2023, with energy prices up almost 21% and core inflation at 2.5%. Super Macro Briefing expects the ECB to raise its main rate by a quarter point to 2.75% in December; Investomine estimates the December hike probability at about 60% (approximate). September producer prices rose 11.1% on the year, composite PMI slipped to 51.7 from 53.1, and retail volumes fell 0.7% on the month (Investomine). The German 10-year Bund yield was around 3.25%, and EUR/USD closed at 1.1146†.

Bank of England. The Bank raised Bank Rate by 25 basis points to 4.00% on 5 November, its first rise since 2023, with one member voting for a 50-basis-point move, Investment Week reported. The rest of the article is behind a paywall, so the reasons and inflation figures are not summarised here. The FTSE 100 fell 0.52% on the week (Investomine†).

Markets. Europe held up far better than the US or Asia: CAC 40 +2.13%, STOXX 600 +0.24%, DAX flat, FTSE 100 −0.52% over the week (Investomine†). TTF gas fell 17.66% on the week to €85.59 per MWh†.

Ukraine. The US will host Russian and Ukrainian delegations in Abu Dhabi on 10–11 November, with US envoys Steve Witkoff and Jared Kushner leading for Washington; the agenda covers post-war security arrangements, de-escalation, humanitarian steps and ending the war, Khaleej Times reported. The two sides remain far apart. Separately, US negotiators meet European and NATO officials in Brussels on security guarantees. Per Super Macro Briefing, the framework keeps NATO's central role, which conflicts with Moscow's demand to roll back post-1997 expansion, and Russia again rejected foreign troops in Ukraine.

Germany leads a modest recovery. Germany's Q3 GDP rose 0.2% on the quarter, a small upside surprise; September industrial production rose 0.7% (August revised to 1.0%), factory orders jumped 4.4%, and capital-goods output rose 3.0%. Energy-intensive output is still down 1.9% on the year. MUFG Research forecasts German growth of 1.1% in 2026 and 1.3% in 2027, which would make Germany outperform France, Italy and the euro area for the first time since 2017, helped by defence and infrastructure spending.

France: weak growth and a bond market on edge. France's GDP shrank in Q3 and the October PMI was revised sharply lower (MUFG). The 10-year OAT yield was 4.95% on 3 November, 4.8% on 4 November and below 4.8% on 5 November, tracking US Treasuries lower. On 20 October the gap over German Bunds was about 147 basis points, which Trading Economics called the widest since the euro-area debt crisis. The same page reports nationwide anti-government protests with curfews in several cities, a Moody's review, Fitch moving France's outlook to negative, and doubts about passing the 2027 budget before the April 2027 presidential election. It also says the revenue section of the budget was due for a National Assembly vote; I could not confirm the result.

Italy, UK and the ECB. Italy's manufacturing PMI fell further below 50 in October, to its lowest since the US–Iran conflict began (MUFG). UK Q3 GDP is due on Thursday, with MUFG and consensus at +0.7% q/q against +0.5% before; the Bank of England estimates that AI-related activity has supplied about a fifth of UK growth this year. MUFG says lower energy prices reduce the risk that the ECB must tighten far beyond what it expects, and that ECB and BoE speakers this week will be read for signs the equity sell-off and the oil retreat changed the December outlook. The euro has recently weakened, which helps exporters. MUFG puts Brent below $100 and TTF gas below €90 per MWh.

Sanctions. The European Commission proposed a 21st package of sanctions on Russia on 9 June 2026: 30 more shadow-fleet vessels (total 662), transaction bans on 31 more Russian banks, a first full third-country ban on crypto-asset services, first-ever fisheries restrictions, and new export limits on metals and drone-related items, per Brussels Times. A July headline from Eurointegration says Ukraine welcomed an agreement on the package, but I could not read it to see the final terms.

Asia-Pacific

Japan, South Korea and Taiwan. Asian chip and AI stocks were hit hardest on Friday. Japan's Nikkei 225 closed down 5.96% at 57,435, its fifth straight fall and worst week since 2020, with all 33 TOPIX sectors lower, WION reported. South Korea's Kospi triggered a 20-minute circuit breaker after opening down more than 8%, and Taiwan fell more than 8% at one point. Samsung Electronics fell about 10%, SK Hynix about 12%, TSMC as much as 8.5% and SoftBank 11%, with the cost of insuring SoftBank's debt at a four-year high. Closing figures for Kospi differ between outlets, so none is quoted. Bloomberg's headline confirms the Nikkei broke below 60,000 (article behind paywall).

Australia. The Reserve Bank of Australia raised the cash rate by 25 basis points to 4.85% on 3 November, the highest since November 2008 and the fifth hike of 2026, in a 6–3 vote. Underlying inflation was 3.7% in September, and the Board said further tightening is warranted. The next meeting is 7–8 December, and banks are split on whether it brings another hike (Savings.com.au). The ASX 200 slipped only 0.5% to 9,020.80 on Friday (WION).

China. September inflation was 0.9% and producer prices 5.0% on the year. This week brings October CPI and PPI on Monday (forecasts: CPI 0.8%, PPI 5.2%), trade data on Tuesday, and money and credit data on Friday, per Lundgreen's Investor Insights. The preview describes weak consumer prices alongside firmer producer prices supported by high crude, squeezing margins. The same preview shows MSCI China up 7.1% as of 5 November (it does not state the period).

India

Markets. The Sensex closed at 81,028.02, up 1,718 points (2.17%), and the Nifty 50 at 25,030.35, up 494 points (2.01%), on Friday, helped by foreign buying, moderate crude and festive Dhanteras demand, ETV Bharat and Kotak Neo report. Mid- and small-caps lagged (midcap −0.02% to −0.54%, smallcap −0.82% to −0.87% depending on source). Sector moves at the close: IT +0.43%, metals +0.66%, PSU banks +0.60%, oil and gas +0.64%. RBI, inflation and rates. The RBI raised the repo rate by 25 basis points to 5.50% on 7 October in a 6–0 vote and shifted its stance to "calibrated tightening" by 4–2; the minutes say rate cuts are off the table in the near term. It projects 2026–27 GDP growth of 7.1% and CPI inflation of 5.2%, with the October–December quarter at 6.0%. The next meeting is 2–4 December (CorpLawUpdates, which cites the RBI release but was not checked against it). Retail inflation was 5.54% in September (CNBC headline); ICRA expects October CPI at 6.1%, driven by food prices, El Niño damage and an unfavourable base effect, NewsBytes reported. The official figure is due 12 November.

Rupee and bonds. The rupee closed at 94.47 per dollar on 5 November, its fifth straight gain, helped by a weaker dollar, lower US yields, foreign inflows and curbs on speculative derivatives bets. The 10-year yield fell to 7.17%, down 24 basis points in November, its lowest since 29 September (Business Standard). Bank of Baroda Research, in a note published before the fall, expected the yield to stay between 7.25% and 7.35% in November with risks skewed up; it had risen from 7.19% to 7.41% in October and system liquidity surplus fell to ₹2.4 lakh crore from ₹7.8 lakh crore (ANI).

Fuel. Petrol and diesel prices have been frozen for thirteen months (Delhi ₹102.12 and ₹95.20, Mumbai ₹111.21 and ₹97.83). State-run oil marketing companies are reported to be losing money on each litre at current prices; the Indian crude basket fell to $103.8 from an October average of $119.9, giving them room to repair margins rather than cut prices, HDFC Sky reported.

Deals and corporate

Germany to take half of KNDS. Germany is said to be buying the families' 50% stake in the Franco-German tank maker for about €6.5 billion, Bloomberg reported (headline only; paywalled). The deal reduces the likelihood of an IPO, according to a newsletter summary you shared.

Shield AI buys Echodyne. The defence-technology startup agreed to acquire the radar maker for $5.3 billion alongside a $2.5 billion fundraise that values it at $28.8 billion, per Washington Technology (headline only).

Adani Ports buys Evos. Adani Ports and SEZ will buy 100% of Netherlands-based Evos from Igneo Infrastructure Partners for an enterprise value of €2.56 billion (about $2.9 billion) and an equity value of €1.18 billion, all cash, The Week reported. Evos has eight terminals in the Netherlands, Germany, Spain, Belgium and Malta, 2025 revenue of €392 million and EBITDA of €219 million. The deal is debt-funded with up to €2.8 billion committed by MUFG, taking Adani Ports' pro forma net debt to EBITDA to 2.76 times, easing to about 2.5 times by FY27. It needs regulatory approvals and has no completion date.
